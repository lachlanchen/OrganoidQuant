# PIBM 2026 Reframed Abstract

## Recommended Title

Quantitative brightfield imaging of droplet-engineered appendix-derived gut organoids for AI-ready optical phenotyping

## Abstract

Quantitative analysis of organoid morphodynamics remains limited by the structural heterogeneity of conventional dome culture, which complicates reproducible optical readout and automated image interpretation. Here, we present a droplet-engineered organoid (DEO) platform using human appendix-derived gut organoids as an optically accessible microdroplet system for longitudinal brightfield imaging and computational phenotyping. Organoid-derived crypt fragments were encapsulated in approximately 500 um Matrigel droplets, enabling standardized time-series observation in a confined geometry with reduced matrix use relative to conventional culture.

Across controlled microenvironmental perturbations, DEOs generated distinct image phenotypes that were quantitatively resolved by segmentation-derived and signal-derived metrics. Time-resolved image analysis captured differences in cyst formation, wall thickening, darkening, shape irregularity, and fusion-associated internal edge redistribution, demonstrating that droplet-scale physical regulation can shift organoid structural states in a measurable manner. Middle- to high-density droplets promoted faster expansion and stronger interaction, whereas mechanically modulated conditions preserved cystic morphology and delayed differentiation-associated structural changes. Additional perturbation increased fusion efficiency while maintaining imaging-compatible cystic structures. Selected image-derived trends were supported by molecular readouts, indicating that the optical phenotypes reflect biologically meaningful state transitions.

These results establish DEOs as a standardized platform for quantitative organoid imaging, image processing, and computational phenotyping. Beyond the current brightfield workflow, the framework is well suited for AI-assisted feature extraction, multimodal optical sensing, and future integration with label-free spectral methods such as hyperspectral imaging, thereby supporting diagnostic-oriented organoid screening and biomarker discovery in biomedical photonics.
